🌿 Neem (Azadirachta indica)

Explore the botanical characteristics, traditional uses, scientific research, and safety information about Neem.

Scientific Name

Azadirachta indica

Family

Meliaceae

Common Names

English: Neem
Swahili: Mwarobaini
French: Margousier

🌱 Botanical Description

Azadirachta indica, commonly known as Neem, is a fast-growing evergreen tree that can reach 15–20 meters in height. It has compound green leaves, fragrant white flowers, and olive-like fruits. The tree is well adapted to tropical and semi-arid climates.

🌍 Distribution

Neem is native to the Indian subcontinent and is now widely cultivated in Africa, including Tanzania, Kenya, Uganda, Burundi, Ethiopia, Nigeria and many other tropical countries.

🧪 Main Active Compounds

  • Azadirachtin
  • Nimbin
  • Nimbidin
  • Quercetin
  • Gedunin
  • Limonoids

🌿 Traditional Uses

  • Leaves are traditionally used in herbal preparations.
  • Twigs have traditionally been used for oral hygiene in some communities.
  • Neem oil has traditional uses for skin and hair care.
  • The tree has also been used traditionally in agriculture for natural pest management.

🔬 Scientific Research

Neem contains many biologically active compounds that have been widely studied. Laboratory research has investigated antimicrobial, insecticidal and anti-inflammatory properties of some neem constituents. More clinical research is needed for many traditional medicinal uses.

⚠️ Safety Information

  • Neem products should be used according to their intended purpose.
  • Neem oil should not be swallowed unless under appropriate medical guidance.
  • Pregnant or breastfeeding women should consult a qualified healthcare professional before using neem products.
  • Traditional remedies should not replace prescribed medical treatment.
