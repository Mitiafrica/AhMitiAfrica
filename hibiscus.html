🌺 Hibiscus (Hibiscus sabdariffa)

Discover the botanical characteristics, traditional uses, scientific research and safety information about Hibiscus.

Scientific Name

Hibiscus sabdariffa

Family

Malvaceae

Common Names

English: Roselle, Hibiscus
Swahili: Rosela
French: Oseille de Guinée

🌱 Botanical Description

Hibiscus sabdariffa, commonly known as Roselle, is an annual shrub that grows up to 2–3 meters tall. It has green to reddish stems, deeply lobed leaves and attractive cream or pale yellow flowers with a dark red center. Its fleshy red calyces are widely harvested for food and beverages.

🌍 Distribution

Hibiscus sabdariffa is cultivated in many tropical and subtropical countries, including Sudan, Egypt, Nigeria, Tanzania, Uganda, Kenya, Burundi and several West African countries.

🧪 Main Active Compounds

  • Anthocyanins
  • Flavonoids
  • Vitamin C
  • Organic acids
  • Polyphenols
  • Natural antioxidants

🌿 Traditional Uses

  • Prepared as a refreshing herbal tea.
  • Used traditionally in foods and beverages.
  • Used in jams, syrups and juices.
  • Traditionally valued in many African communities.
  • Sometimes used as a natural food colouring.

🔬 Scientific Research

Hibiscus has been studied for its antioxidant properties and nutritional composition. Some clinical studies have explored its potential effects on blood pressure and cholesterol, but more research is needed before firm medical conclusions can be made.

⚠️ Safety Information

  • Generally safe when consumed as a food or beverage.
  • People taking medication for blood pressure or diabetes should consult a healthcare professional before using concentrated herbal preparations.
  • Pregnant or breastfeeding women should seek professional advice before medicinal use.
  • Herbal products should not replace prescribed medical treatment.
