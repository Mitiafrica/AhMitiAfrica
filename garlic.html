🧄 Garlic (Allium sativum)

Discover the botanical characteristics, traditional uses, scientific research and safety information about Garlic.

Scientific Name

Allium sativum

Family

Amaryllidaceae

Common Names

English: Garlic
Swahili: Kitunguu Saumu
French: Ail

🌱 Botanical Description

Allium sativum, commonly known as garlic, is a perennial bulb-forming plant belonging to the Amaryllidaceae family. It has long, flat leaves and produces white to pink flowers. The underground bulb is made up of several cloves, which are the part most commonly used.

🌍 Distribution

Garlic is cultivated throughout the world, including many African countries such as Egypt, Ethiopia, Kenya, Tanzania, Uganda, Rwanda, Burundi and Nigeria. It grows best in well-drained fertile soils under cool to warm conditions.

🧪 Main Active Compounds

  • Allicin
  • Alliin
  • Ajoene
  • Diallyl sulfides
  • Selenium
  • Flavonoids
  • Vitamin C

🌿 Traditional Uses

  • Widely used as a culinary spice.
  • Traditionally included in herbal preparations in many cultures.
  • Commonly used to flavor food.
  • Traditionally used in home remedies for general wellness.
  • Used in many traditional African, Asian and Mediterranean practices.

🔬 Scientific Research

Garlic has been widely studied for its sulfur-containing compounds, particularly allicin. Research has investigated antioxidant, antimicrobial and cardiovascular effects of garlic. While some findings are promising, further high-quality clinical research is needed for many health claims.

⚠️ Safety Information

  • Garlic is generally safe when consumed as food.
  • Large amounts may cause stomach irritation in some people.
  • Garlic supplements may interact with blood-thinning medicines.
  • Consult a healthcare professional before taking concentrated garlic supplements, especially before surgery or if taking prescription medicines.
  • Garlic should not replace prescribed medical treatment.
