🌿 Cloves (Syzygium aromaticum)

Learn about cloves, their botanical characteristics, traditional uses, scientific research, and safety information.

Scientific Name

Syzygium aromaticum

Family

Myrtaceae

Common Names

English: Cloves
Swahili: Karafuu
French: Girofle

🌱 Botanical Description

Cloves are the dried flower buds of the evergreen tree Syzygium aromaticum. The tree can grow between 8 and 15 meters tall and produces aromatic flower buds that are harvested before opening and dried naturally.

🌍 Distribution

Cloves are native to the Maluku Islands of Indonesia but are now cultivated in many tropical regions including Tanzania, Zanzibar, Madagascar, Comoros and other parts of Africa.

🧪 Main Active Compounds

  • Eugenol
  • Eugenyl acetate
  • Beta-caryophyllene
  • Flavonoids
  • Tannins

🌿 Traditional Uses

  • Relieving toothache
  • Supporting digestion
  • Used as a culinary spice
  • Freshening breath
  • Traditional herbal preparations

🔬 Scientific Research

Scientific studies have shown that cloves contain compounds with antioxidant and antimicrobial activity. Research is ongoing to better understand their potential uses in medicine. Cloves should not be considered a replacement for professional medical treatment.

⚠️ Safety Information

  • Use in moderation.
  • Essential clove oil should be used carefully.
  • Keep away from small children unless advised by a healthcare professional.
  • Consult a healthcare professional before using cloves for medical purposes, especially during pregnancy or when taking medications.
